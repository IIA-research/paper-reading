**when: In the training stage, by a predetermined method (here, the start of every conversation), it rolls out asking or not asking to collect ground truth, and uses this to learn a controller; then in the prediction stage it relies on the controller's scores. what: a template plus attribute words decided by the task (that is, the questions most likely to be asked), then one choice among 6 questions + not asking.**

In detail, in the training stage: A task comes in. Right after the user's question, candidate topics are chosen. The candidates come from attributes that come with the dataset, or are generated by a frozen LLM (the questions this task is most likely to leave out and most needs to ask). Here (that is, at the start of the task, after the user's question), 5 rollouts are split off: 2 do the task directly without asking, 2 ask about the one topic z the controller likes most, and 1 asks about another topic to force exploration. Each runs to the end and gets a final score. "Average score of asking z − average score of not asking" is the value of z. This number does two things: it is the regression label for the controller, and it is the advantage of this continuation used to update the policy. Each task is used only once in training. The controller takes in the embedding of the question, so it has some sense of meaning. At first its outputs are all 0 (random start), and at first it picks by instinct from the embedding; only after 64 tasks does it start to have an opinion. The controller is a frozen 4B model + an MLP with 670,000 parameters. What it learns is "for a request like this, and a topic that can be asked like this, how many points it usually adds".

In the testing stage: At the start, the controller scores every topic. If the top score is > 0, it asks; after asking, the conversation is updated and the scores are recomputed, until no remaining topic has a score above 0. Then the agent finishes the task by itself according to its policy.

- Data: UserGym (Travel, Turtle, Intention, Telepathy: four tasks with a simulated user), τ²-Bench (airline, retail and telecom customer service), IN3 (judge whether an instruction is missing information), AskBench (questions with missing information or a false premise).
- Baselines: prompting (Vanilla, ReAct, Reflexion, Proactive CoT, PRIME); threshold heuristics (CLAM, INTENT-SIM, and a threshold version of VoI made by the authors); information-gain training (IG-Clarifier, InfoPO, IGPO); SFT, GRPO; the appendix also compares ACT and CollabLLM.
